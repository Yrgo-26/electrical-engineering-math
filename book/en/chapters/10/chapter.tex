\chapter{Exponential functions, logarithms and decibels}
\label{c10:ch}

{\large\itshape How to solve for an unknown exponent, and why gain is calculated in
decibels.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Exponential functions and exponential equations.
  \item Logarithmic functions: the base-10 logarithm and the natural logarithm.
  \item The laws of logarithms.
  \item Decibels.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

When the unknown number sits in an exponent, as in $2^x = 64$, the operations we have used so far
are not enough. This chapter shows how logarithms solve such equations: a logarithm turns the
exponent into a factor, which can then be solved for. The same tool describes quantities that
decrease or grow by a fixed factor per unit of time, for example the charge in a battery. Last
come decibels, the logarithmic scale electrical engineering uses for gain and attenuation, where
the amplifier stages in a chain are added instead of multiplied.

\Secref{c10:sec:exponential} to \secref{c10:sec:typexempel} are the chapter's theory, with
examples. \Secref{c10:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the formulas and what you should be able to
do after the chapter, and \secref{c10:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises, where Part~1 reviews the
trigonometry of \kapref{9}. For many of them you need a calculator with logarithms. You may like to
read Wikipedia's article on the decibel, \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel}, before you
start.
