\chapter{Complex numbers, part I}
\label{c15:ch}

{\large\itshape A number whose square is negative, and how it describes voltages and currents.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What complex numbers are and how to calculate with them.
  \item Rectangular form and the complex plane.
  \item Magnitude and phase angle, with quadrant correction.
  \item Conversion between rectangular and polar form.
  \item Applications in electrical engineering: voltage, current and impedance.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

The equation $x^2 = -1$ has no solution among the real numbers. Introducing the imaginary unit
$j$, with $j^2 = -1$, extends the numbers to the complex numbers. This chapter shows how a complex
number is written in rectangular form and drawn in the complex plane, how its magnitude and phase
angle are calculated, and how it is converted between rectangular and polar form. In electrical
engineering, complex numbers are the natural way to calculate with AC voltages and alternating
currents, which have both a size and a phase angle.

\Secref{c15:sec:inledning} to \secref{c15:sec:rakneoperationer} are the chapter's theory, and
\secref{c15:sec:typexempel} gives three worked examples. \Secref{c15:sec:sammanfattning}
summarises the formulas and what you should be able to do after the chapter, and
\secref{c15:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. You will need a calculator for the phase angles.
