\chapter{Derivatives, part I}
\label{c12:ch}

{\large\itshape How fast a quantity changes, the slope of the tangent, and the peaks and valleys of
a curve.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rate of change and the tangent.
  \item The definition of the derivative.
  \item The derivatives of power and polynomial functions.
  \item Zeros of the derivative, maximum and minimum points.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

The derivative describes how fast a quantity changes. In electrical engineering it turns up
directly in circuit theory:
\[
  i_C(t) = C\frac{du_C}{dt}, \qquad u_L(t) = L\frac{di_L}{dt}
\]
The current through a capacitor depends on how fast the voltage across it changes, and the voltage
across an inductor depends on how fast the current through it changes. The derivative is therefore
not abstract mathematics, but the tool that describes transients in RC and RL circuits. This
chapter builds up the concept from the difference quotient to the slope of the tangent, gives the
rules of differentiation for polynomials and shows how the derivative finds the maximum and minimum
points of a function. A short introduction to the derivative as a concept is in the video
\url{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N2PpRnFqnqY}.

\Secref{c12:sec:andringskvot} to \secref{c12:sec:typexempel} are the chapter's theory, with
examples. \Secref{c12:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the rules and what you should be able to do
after the chapter, and \secref{c12:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. Part~1 of them reviews the
decibel calculations from \kapref{10}, which the practice test in \secref{c11:sec:ovningsdugga}
also covers.
