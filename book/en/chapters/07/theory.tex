\section{Coordinate systems and distances}
\label{c7:sec:koordinatsystem}
A \textbf{coordinate system} (the $xy$-plane) has a horizontal $x$-axis and a vertical $y$-axis
that meet at the origin $(0, 0)$.

The distance $d$ between two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is given by Pythagoras'
theorem:
\[
  d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
\]
The angle that the line segment makes with the positive $x$-axis is
\[
  \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\right).
\]

\begin{aside}
\note[Note] Always check which quadrant the point is in and correct the angle if needed:
$+180^{\circ}$ for quadrants II and III.
\end{aside}

\section{Vectors -- concepts and notation}
\label{c7:sec:begrepp}
A \textbf{vector} represents a quantity with both magnitude and direction. In a plane, the vector
$\mathbf{u}$ is written with its components:
\[
  \mathbf{u} = (u_x, u_y)
\]
Unlike a scalar, a vector carries information about direction. In electrical engineering, vectors
are used to represent voltages and currents in AC systems.

\section{Magnitude and angle}
\label{c7:sec:absolutbelopp}
The \textbf{magnitude} (length) of $\mathbf{u} = (u_x, u_y)$ is
\[
  \abs{\mathbf{u}} = \sqrt{u_x^2 + u_y^2}.
\]
The \textbf{angle} $v_u$ with the positive $x$-axis is
\[
  v_u = \arctan\left(\frac{u_y}{u_x}\right) + \text{quadrant correction},
\]
where the quadrant correction is given by the table.

\begin{narrowtable}{@{}cccl@{}}
  \thead{Quadrant} & $u_x$ & $u_y$ & \thead{Correction} \\
  \midrule
  I & $+$ & $+$ & none \\
  II & $-$ & $+$ & $+180^{\circ}$ \\
  III & $-$ & $-$ & $+180^{\circ}$ \\
  IV & $+$ & $-$ & none (negative angle) \\
\end{narrowtable}

\section{The angle between two vectors}
\label{c7:sec:vinkel}
The angle $\alpha$ between $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is the difference between the vectors'
angles with the positive $x$-axis. So first calculate $v_u$ and $v_v$ separately, and then the
difference:
\[
  \alpha = v_v - v_u
\]
The angle between two vectors is given as a value between $0^{\circ}$ and $180^{\circ}$. If the
difference is negative, change its sign, and if it is greater than $180^{\circ}$, subtract it
from $360^{\circ}$.

\section{Calculating with vectors}
\label{c7:sec:rakna}

\subsection{Addition and subtraction}
\label{c7:sec:addition}
\[
  \mathbf{u} \pm \mathbf{v} = (u_x \pm v_x,\; u_y \pm v_y)
\]

\subsection{Multiplication by a number}
\label{c7:sec:multiplikation}
When a vector is multiplied by a number $k$, both components are multiplied by the number:
\[
  k\mathbf{u} = (k \cdot u_x,\; k \cdot u_y)
\]
For example, for $\mathbf{U} = (3, 4)$,
\[
  2\mathbf{U} = (2 \cdot 3,\; 2 \cdot 4) = (6, 8).
\]
A negative number also reverses the direction of the vector.

\section{Worked example}
\label{c7:sec:typexempel}

\begin{exempel}[Vectors in a coordinate system]
Two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are shown in the figure below.

\bild[0.4\linewidth]{lectures/L07/appendix/images/example1_1.png}

\begin{parts}
  \item Find the coordinates of the vectors.
  \item Calculate the magnitudes $\abs{\mathbf{u}}$ and $\abs{\mathbf{v}}$ of the vectors.
  \item Calculate the angles $v_u$ and $v_v$ of the vectors.
  \item Calculate the angle between the vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$.
  \item Calculate a third vector $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{u} + 2\mathbf{v}$.
  \item Determine a vector of length $5$ that points in the opposite direction to $\mathbf{u}$.
\end{parts}

\textbf{a)} Looking at the figure, we see that the vector $\mathbf{u}$ has the $x$-coordinate
$3$ and the $y$-coordinate $3$. So
\[
  \mathbf{u} = (3, 3)
\]
We also see that the vector $\mathbf{v}$ has the $x$-coordinate $-2$ and the $y$-coordinate $3$.
So
\[
  \mathbf{v} = (-2, 3)
\]

\textbf{b)} The magnitude of each vector equals its hypotenuse and is therefore easily
calculated with Pythagoras' theorem.

This can be visualised by drawing each vector as a triangle, see the figure below.

\bild[0.4\linewidth]{lectures/L07/appendix/images/example1_2.png}

The magnitudes $\abs{\mathbf{u}}$ and $\abs{\mathbf{v}}$ are calculated as follows:
\begin{gather*}
  \abs{\mathbf{u}} = \sqrt{3^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{18} \approx 4.24 \\
  \abs{\mathbf{v}} = \sqrt{3^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{13} \approx 3.61
\end{gather*}

\textbf{c)} To calculate the angles of the vectors we can use the tangent. Once again we see
the vectors as triangles.

For the vector $\mathbf{u} = (3, 3)$, the opposite and the adjacent side are both equal to $3$.
So
\[
  \tan(v_u) = \frac{3}{3} = 1,
\]
which can be transformed into
\[
  v_u = \arctan(1) = 45^{\circ}
\]
For the vector $\mathbf{v}$, which has a negative $x$-value, the corresponding triangle can be
drawn as shown in the figure above. Note that in this case we have marked an angle $v_x$, which
is the supplementary angle of $v_v$:
\[
  v_v + v_x = 180^{\circ},
\]
which means that
\[
  v_v = 180^{\circ} - v_x
\]
If we calculate the angle $v_x$, we can therefore easily calculate the angle $v_v$. The angle
$v_x$ is easily calculated with the tangent, since the corresponding triangle has an opposite
side equal to $3$ and an adjacent side equal to $2$. So
\[
  \tan(v_x) = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \quad \Rightarrow \quad
  v_x = \arctan(1.5) \approx 56.3^{\circ}
\]
We can then easily calculate the angle $v_v$:
\[
  v_v = 180^{\circ} - v_x = 180^{\circ} - 56.3^{\circ} = 123.7^{\circ}
\]

\textbf{d)} To calculate the angle $\alpha$ between $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{u}$, we calculate
the difference between their respective angles $v_v$ and $v_u$:
\[
  \alpha = v_v - v_u
\]
We insert the values into the formula and see that the angle between the vectors is about
$78.7^{\circ}$, since
\[
  \alpha = v_v - v_u = 123.7^{\circ} - 45^{\circ} \approx 78.7^{\circ}
\]

\textbf{e)} We calculate the vector $\mathbf{w}$ algebraically by inserting the values of the
vectors $\mathbf{u} = (3, 3)$ and $\mathbf{v} = (-2, 3)$ into the given formula:
\[
  \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{u} + 2\mathbf{v} = (3, 3) + 2(-2, 3),
\]
where
\[
  2(-2, 3) = (2 \cdot (-2),\; 2 \cdot 3) = (-4, 6)
\]
So
\[
  \mathbf{w} = (3, 3) + (-4, 6)
\]
To complete the calculation we add the $x$- and $y$-values and see that
$\mathbf{w} = (-1, 9)$, since
\[
  \mathbf{w} = (3 - 4,\; 3 + 6) = (-1, 9)
\]

\textbf{f)} We can draw a vector of length $5$ in the opposite direction to $\mathbf{u}$. Let us
call this vector $\mathbf{a}$. That the vector points the opposite way means that it is turned
$180^{\circ}$, that is half a turn, from $\mathbf{u}$. For its angle $v_a$ we therefore have
\[
  v_a = v_u + 180^{\circ}
\]
Since the angle $v_u$ was calculated earlier to be $45^{\circ}$, the angle $v_a$ of the vector
$\mathbf{a}$ is $225^{\circ}$, since
\[
  v_a = 45^{\circ} + 180^{\circ} = 225^{\circ}
\]
The length of a given vector equals its magnitude. So the magnitude $\abs{\mathbf{a}}$ of the
vector $\mathbf{a}$ is $5$:
\[
  \abs{\mathbf{a}} = 5
\]
We can then calculate the vector $\mathbf{a}$ by two methods, graphically or algebraically.

\textbf{Method 1 -- Graphical solution.} As we saw earlier, the vector can be drawn as a
triangle whose hypotenuse equals its magnitude. Since this vector is turned half a turn from
the vector $\mathbf{u}$, we can draw the vector $\mathbf{a}$ as a triangle with the angle
$45^{\circ}$ seen from $180^{\circ}$, that is, turned half a turn.

With this information we can draw the vector $\mathbf{a}$ as shown below.

\bild[0.4\linewidth]{lectures/L07/appendix/images/example1_3.png}

We can calculate the magnitude $\abs{x_a}$ of the adjacent side $x_a$ of the vector
$\mathbf{a}$ with the cosine:
\[
  \cos 45^{\circ} = \frac{\abs{x_a}}{5},
\]
which means that
\[
  \abs{x_a} = 5 \cdot \cos 45^{\circ} \approx 3.54
\]
Since $x_a$ lies to the left of the origin, $x_a$ is negative. So
\[
  x_a \approx -3.54
\]
We can also calculate the magnitude $\abs{y_a}$ of the opposite side $y_a$ of the vector
$\mathbf{a}$ with the sine:
\[
  \sin 45^{\circ} = \frac{\abs{y_a}}{5},
\]
which means that
\[
  \abs{y_a} = 5 \cdot \sin 45^{\circ} \approx 3.54
\]
Since $y_a$ lies below the origin, $y_a$ is negative. So
\[
  y_a \approx -3.54
\]
From the calculated values the vector $\mathbf{a}$ can then be determined:
\[
  \mathbf{a} \approx (-3.54,\; -3.54)
\]

\textbf{Method 2 -- Algebraic solution.} We saw earlier that the angle $v_a$ of the vector
$\mathbf{a}$ is $225^{\circ}$, since it is turned half a turn ($180^{\circ}$) from the vector
$\mathbf{u}$, whose angle $v_u$ was calculated earlier to be $45^{\circ}$:
\[
  v_a = v_u + 180^{\circ} = 45^{\circ} + 180^{\circ} = 225^{\circ}
\]
The magnitude $\abs{\mathbf{a}}$ of the vector, which equals its length, was also specified as
$5$ in the problem:
\[
  \abs{\mathbf{a}} = 5
\]
We can easily calculate the $x$- and $y$-values of the vector with the cosine and sine. We
begin by calculating the $x$-value $x_a$ of the vector:
\[
  \cos v_a = \frac{x_a}{\abs{\mathbf{a}}},
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
  x_a = \abs{\mathbf{a}} \cdot \cos v_a
\]
By inserting values into the formula above we see that the $x$-value $x_a$ of the vector is
$-3.54$, since
\[
  x_a = 5 \cdot \cos 225^{\circ} \approx -3.54
\]
We can then calculate the $y$-value $y_a$ of the vector $\mathbf{a}$ with the sine:
\[
  \sin v_a = \frac{y_a}{\abs{\mathbf{a}}},
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
  y_a = \abs{\mathbf{a}} \cdot \sin v_a
\]
By inserting values into the formula above we see that the $y$-value $y_a$ of the vector
$\mathbf{a}$ is also $-3.54$, since
\[
  y_a = 5 \cdot \sin 225^{\circ} \approx -3.54
\]
So
\[
  \mathbf{a} \approx (-3.54,\; -3.54)
\]
\end{exempel}
