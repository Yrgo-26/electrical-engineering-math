\chapter{Vectors}
\label{c7:ch}

{\large\itshape Quantities with both magnitude and direction, and how to calculate with them.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Distances and angles in a coordinate system.
  \item Vectors: concepts and notation.
  \item Magnitude, angle and the scalar product.
  \item Calculations with vectors: addition, subtraction and multiplication by a scalar.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

A number such as a resistance or a temperature has only a size. Many quantities also have a
direction: a force pulls one particular way, and in AC circuits voltages and currents are
described by a magnitude and a phase angle. Such quantities are described by vectors. This chapter
begins with distances and angles in a coordinate system and then goes through vectors: how they
are written with components, how their length and angle are calculated, and how they are added,
subtracted and multiplied by a number. The scalar product also gives the angle between two
vectors. Vectors return later in the book, when voltages and currents are handled as phasors and
complex numbers.

\Secref{c7:sec:koordinatsystem} to \secref{c7:sec:rakna} are the chapter's theory, and
\secref{c7:sec:typexempel} shows it in three worked examples. \Secref{c7:sec:sammanfattning}
summarises the formulas and what you should be able to do after the chapter, and
\secref{c7:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises.
