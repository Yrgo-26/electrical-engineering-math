\chapter{Quadratic equations}
\label{c6:ch}

{\large\itshape When the unknown is squared: two roots, one or none.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Quadratic equations and their solutions.
  \item The discriminant, which decides how many real roots an equation has.
  \item The factorisation method, the PQ-formula and the ABC-formula.
  \item Completing the square, and Vieta's formulas.
  \item Current from power, and resistances from their sum and product.
  \item Practice test 1, a review of Chapters 1--6.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

So far the unknown has appeared to the first power, and the equations could be solved by isolating
the unknown step by step. In a quadratic equation the unknown is squared, and that is no longer
enough. This chapter shows how such an equation is solved: by factorisation, with the PQ-formula
and the ABC-formula, and by completing the square, which is how the formulas are derived. The
discriminant decides whether the equation has two, one or no real roots, and Vieta's formulas give
a quick check of the answer. In electrical engineering, quadratic equations arise when, for
example, the current is to be calculated from the power $P = RI^2$, or two resistors are to be
found from their sum and product.

\Secref{c6:sec:definition} to \secref{c6:sec:tillampning} are the chapter's theory, with
examples. \Secref{c6:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the methods and what you should be able to do
after the chapter, and \secref{c6:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. The chapter closes with
practice test~1 in \secref{c6:sec:ovningsdugga}, which reviews Chapters 1--6 before the first
test.
