\chapter{Powers and roots}
\label{c5:ch}

{\large\itshape Repeated multiplication, roots, powers of ten and how many digits an answer should
have.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Powers and the laws of exponents.
  \item Square roots and $n$th roots.
  \item Scientific notation (standard form) and SI prefixes.
  \item Significant figures and rounding.
  \item Calculating electrical power with powers and powers of ten.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

In electrical engineering, most values are either very large or very small: a resistance of a few
megohms and a capacitance of a few picofarads can sit in the same circuit. This chapter goes
through powers and the rules that apply to them, square roots and $n$th roots, and how roots can
be written as powers with fractional exponents. Numbers are then written in scientific notation
and with SI prefixes, and you learn to calculate with powers of ten and to give a result with a
sensible number of significant figures. The chapter ends with calculating electrical power, where
both squares and powers of ten are needed.

\Secref{c5:sec:potenser} to \secref{c5:sec:effekt} are the chapter's theory, with examples.
\Secref{c5:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the rules and what you should be able to do after the
chapter, and \secref{c5:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. Most of them can be done in your head
or with pencil and paper.
