\chapter{Complex numbers, part III}
\label{c17:ch}

{\large\itshape Sinusoidal signals as phasors, and impedance as a complex number.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Complex numbers for calculations with sinusoidal signals.
  \item Converting between a sinusoidal signal and a phasor.
  \item Phasor addition in five steps.
  \item Multiplication and division with phasors.
  \item Impedance of a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter concludes the three chapters on complex numbers. In \kapref{16} you learned that a
sinusoidal signal can be represented by a phasor, a complex number that carries the signal's
amplitude and phase angle. Here the phasors are put to the use they are made for: adding
sinusoidal voltages and currents of the same frequency, and calculating with impedance, where
voltage, current and impedance are related exactly as in Ohm's law, but with complex numbers. You
will also see how the phase angle of the impedance decides whether the current leads or lags the
voltage.

\Secref{c17:sec:fasorer} to \secref{c17:sec:typexempel} are the chapter's theory, with worked
examples. \Secref{c17:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the formulas and what you should be able to
do after the chapter, and \secref{c17:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. The angles are sometimes
given in degrees and sometimes in radians, so keep track of which mode your calculator is in.
