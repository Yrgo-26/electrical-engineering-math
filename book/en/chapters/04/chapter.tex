\chapter{Systems of equations}
\label{c4:ch}

{\large\itshape Several conditions that must hold at once, two ways to solve them, and Kirchhoff's
laws.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Linear systems of equations in two unknowns.
  \item The substitution method.
  \item The elimination method (addition method).
  \item Graphical interpretation of systems of equations.
  \item Kirchhoff's laws, which give systems of equations for the currents and voltages in a
    circuit.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

In \kapref{3} you solved equations in one unknown. A circuit often has several unknown currents
and voltages, and every relationship between them becomes an equation of its own; they must all
hold at once. This chapter is about such systems of equations, primarily two linear equations in
two unknowns. You learn two methods of solving them, the substitution method and the elimination
method, and see what the solution means graphically: the point where two straight lines
intersect, which also decides whether the system has one solution, none at all or infinitely
many. Finally, Kirchhoff's laws are used to set up a system of equations for the currents in a
circuit.

\Secref{c4:sec:ekvationssystem} to \secref{c4:sec:kirchhoff} are the chapter's theory, with
examples. \Secref{c4:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the methods and what you should be able to do
after the chapter, and \secref{c4:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. Most of them can be done in
your head or with pencil and paper.
