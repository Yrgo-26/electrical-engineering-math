\chapter{Derivatives, part II}
\label{c13:ch}

{\large\itshape The derivatives of sine, cosine, exponential functions and logarithms.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The derivatives of trigonometric functions.
  \item The derivatives of exponential functions.
  \item The derivatives of logarithmic functions.
  \item The product rule and the chain rule.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

In \kapref{12} we differentiated polynomials. Many quantities in electrical engineering are not
polynomials, though: alternating currents and voltages are sinusoidal, a capacitor discharges
exponentially and gain is measured with logarithms. This chapter gives the derivatives of
trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions, and the product rule and the chain rule,
which are needed when functions are multiplied together or substituted into each other. With them
you can, for example, calculate the current through a capacitor when the voltage across it is
sinusoidal, or how fast the voltage falls as the capacitor discharges.

\Secref{c13:sec:trig} to \secref{c13:sec:typexempel} are the chapter's theory, with examples.
\Secref{c13:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the rules and what you should be able to do after the
chapter, and \secref{c13:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. Part~1 of them reviews the analysis
of polynomials from \kapref{12}.
