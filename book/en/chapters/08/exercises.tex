\uppgiftsdel{Part 1}{The concept of a function, domain and range}

\uppgift{1.1}{Is the relation a function?}
Decide whether the current $I$ is a function of the voltage $U$. Justify your answer.
\begin{parts}
  \item $I = \dfrac{U}{10}$ (Ohm's law for a $10\,\Omega$ resistor)
  \item The measured pairs $(U, I)$ with $U$ in V and $I$ in mA: $\lbrace(2, 100),\ (4, 200),\ (6,
    250),\ (8, 250)\rbrace$
  \item The measured pairs $(U, I)$ with $U$ in V and $I$ in mA: $\lbrace(1, 2),\ (1, 5),\ (2,
    3)\rbrace$
\end{parts}

\uppgift{1.2}{Domain and range}
Find the domain and the range.
\begin{parts}
  \item The temperature sensor \code{TMP36} gives the output voltage $U(T) = 0.01T + 0.5$ (in V) at
    the temperature $T$ (in \textdegree C). The sensor works for $-40 \leq T \leq 125$.
  \item The voltage across a $100\,\Omega$ resistor that develops the power $P$ (in W) is $U(P) =
    \sqrt{100P}$.
  \item The power in a $10\,\Omega$ resistor is $P(U) = \dfrac{U^2}{10}$, where the voltage can be
    $-20 \leq U \leq 20$ (in V).
\end{parts}

\uppgift{1.3}{Linear resistance of a temperature-dependent resistor}
The resistance $R$ (in $\Omega$) of a certain temperature-dependent resistor depends linearly on
the temperature $T$ (in $^{\circ}$C) as $R(T) = kT + m$. At $T = 0\,^{\circ}\text{C}$,
$R = 100\,\Omega$, and at $T = 50\,^{\circ}\text{C}$, $R = 175\,\Omega$.
\begin{parts}
  \item Find $k$ and $m$.
  \item Calculate the resistance at $T = 25\,^{\circ}\text{C}$.
\end{parts}

\uppgiftsdel{Part 2}{Types of function and applications}

\uppgift{2.1}{An ageing battery}
A new lithium-ion battery has the capacity $4000\,\text{mAh}$ and loses $3\,\%$ of its capacity a
year. After $x$ years the capacity is
\[
  C(x) = 4000 \cdot 0.97^x\,\text{mAh}.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $C(10)$ and interpret the answer.
  \item A battery is usually considered worn out when its capacity has dropped to $80\,\%$. After how
    many years does that happen?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{2.2}{Power as a power function of the current}
The power $P$ (in W) developed in a resistor $R = 50\,\Omega$ depends on the current $I$ (in A)
as
\[
  P(I) = R \cdot I^2.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item What type of function is $P(I)$?
  \item Calculate $P(0.5)$ and $P(2)$.
  \item A fuse in the circuit limits the current to $0 \leq I \leq 3\,\text{A}$. Find the domain
    and range of $P(I)$ in this interval.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{2.3}{Interpreting measurements}
A voltage $U(t)$ is measured at several times during a discharge:

\begin{narrowtable}{@{}l*{5}{>{\centering\arraybackslash}p{2.2em}}@{}}
  $t$ (s) & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ \\
  \midrule
  $U(t)$ (V) & $12$ & $9$ & $6$ & $3$ & $0$ \\
\end{narrowtable}

\begin{parts}
  \item Show from the table that the relationship between $t$ and $U(t)$ is linear.
  \item Find a formula $U(t) = kt + m$ from the table.
  \item If the pattern in the table continued, at what time $t$ would $U(t) = -3\,\text{V}$? Is
    this a reasonable value for a discharge voltage? Explain why the linear model cannot hold for
    all $t \geq 0$.
\end{parts}

\uppgiftsdel{Part 3}{Extra exercises}
The exercises below are extra practice, best done on your own after the lesson.

\uppgift{3.1}{Calculating function values}
A battery has the terminal voltage $U(I) = 12 - 2I$ (in V), a resistor develops the power $P(I) =
5I^2$ (in W), and the current through a load is $I(R) = \dfrac{24}{R}$ (in A). Calculate:
\begin{delar}(3)
  \task $U(1)$
  \task $U(4)$
  \task $P(0)$
  \task $P(3)$
  \task $I(8)$
  \task $I(12)$
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.2}{Solving the equation $f(x) = c$}
The same functions as in exercise~3.1.
\begin{parts}
  \item At what current is the terminal voltage $9\,\text{V}$?
  \item Find the zero of $U(I)$. What does it mean for the battery?
  \item At what current does the resistor develop $20\,\text{W}$?
  \item Can $P(I)$ be negative? Justify your answer.
  \item Which resistance gives the current $0.5\,\text{A}$?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.3}{Domain}
Find the mathematical domain. Then state which values are physically reasonable.
\begin{delar}(2)
  \task $I(R) = \dfrac{10}{R}$
  \task $I(R) = \dfrac{12}{R + 2}$
  \task $U(P) = \sqrt{50P}$
  \task $X(f) = \dfrac{1000}{f}$
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.4}{Range}
Find the range.
\begin{parts}
  \item $P(I) = 10I^2$ (in W) for $0 \leq I \leq 2$ (in A)
  \item $U(I) = 12 - 2I$ (in V) for $0 \leq I \leq 6$ (in A)
  \item A rectifier gives the output voltage $u_{\text{out}} = \abs{u_{\text{in}}}$ for $-10 \leq
    u_{\text{in}} \leq 10$ (in V).
  \item $T(U) = 100U - 50$ (in \textdegree C) for $0.1 \leq U \leq 1.75$ (in V)
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.5}{A linear function from two points}
Find the linear function.
\begin{parts}
  \item A battery has the terminal voltage $9\,\text{V}$ at $0\,\text{A}$ and $8\,\text{V}$ at
    $2\,\text{A}$. Find $U(I)$.
  \item A temperature sensor gives $0.5\,\text{V}$ at $0\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $1.5\,\text{V}$ at
    $100\,^{\circ}\text{C}$. Find $U(T)$.
  \item A Pt100 element has the resistance $100\,\Omega$ at $0\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and
    $138.5\,\Omega$ at $100\,^{\circ}\text{C}$. Find $R(T)$.
  \item A charging current is $2\,\text{A}$ at $t = 0\,\text{s}$ and $2\,\text{A}$ at $t =
    5\,\text{s}$. Find $I(t)$.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.6}{Interpreting $k$ and $m$}
A loaded battery has the terminal voltage $U(I) = 12 - 0.5I$ (in V), where $I$ is the current in
A.
\begin{parts}
  \item What does $k$ mean graphically?
  \item What does $m$ mean graphically?
  \item Is $U(I)$ increasing or decreasing? Justify your answer.
  \item Find the zero of $U(I)$.
  \item What do $k = -0.5$ and $m = 12$ represent physically?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.7}{Power functions}
The power in a $10\,\Omega$ resistor is $P(I) = 10I^2$ (in W).
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $P(3)$ and $P(-3)$. Why is the power the same when the current changes direction?
  \item How many times larger does the power become if the current is tripled?
  \item How does the power change if the current is halved?
  \item The power can also be written $P(U) = \dfrac{U^2}{10}$. Calculate $P(5)$ and $P(10)$. How does
    the power change when the voltage is doubled?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.8}{Exponential functions}
An amplifier chain doubles the signal in each stage, so that $u(n) = 5 \cdot 2^n$ (in mV) after
$n$ stages. An attenuator instead halves the signal in each stage, so that $g(n) = 100 \cdot
0.5^n$ (in mV).
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $u(0)$, $u(1)$ and $u(3)$.
  \item Calculate $g(0)$, $g(1)$ and $g(3)$.
  \item Which of the functions is increasing and which is decreasing? What decides it?
  \item What is $C$ in each function, and what does it mean?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.9}{Growth factor}
\begin{parts}
  \item The price of electricity increases by $5\,\%$ a year. What is the growth factor $a$?
  \item The capacity of a battery decreases by $20\,\%$ a year. What is $a$?
  \item A signal is attenuated to $70\,\%$ of its value for each stage it passes. The initial
    amplitude is $2\,\text{V}$. Write a function for the amplitude after $n$ stages.
  \item Calculate the amplitude after $3$ stages.
  \item After how many stages is the amplitude below $1\,\text{V}$?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.10}{Linear or exponential?}
Two quantities have been measured once a year: $f(x)$ is the number of charging points in a
municipality (in hundreds) and $g(x)$ is the number of connected sensors in a network (in
thousands), where $x$ is the number of years.

\begin{narrowtable}{@{}l*{5}{>{\centering\arraybackslash}p{2.2em}}@{}}
  $x$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ \\
  \midrule
  $f(x)$ & $3$ & $5$ & $7$ & $9$ & $11$ \\
  $g(x)$ & $3$ & $6$ & $12$ & $24$ & $48$ \\
\end{narrowtable}

\begin{parts}
  \item Which of the functions is linear? Justify your answer from the table.
  \item Which is exponential? Justify your answer.
  \item Find a formula for $f(x)$.
  \item Find a formula for $g(x)$.
  \item Calculate $f(10)$ and $g(10)$.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.11}{Zeros}
Find the zeros and interpret them.
\begin{parts}
  \item The terminal voltage $U(I) = 12 - 4I$ (in V) of a battery, where $I$ is the current in A.
  \item The power $P(I) = 12I - 2I^2$ (in W) that the same kind of battery, with the internal
    resistance $2\,\Omega$, delivers to a load.
  \item The voltage $U(t) = 6 - 0.5t$ (in V) during a discharge, where $t$ is the time in s.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.12}{A voltage divider as a function}
A voltage divider consists of $R_1 = 1\,\text{k}\Omega$ and a potentiometer with the resistance
$x$ (in k$\Omega$). The output voltage is given by
\[
  U(x) = 12 \cdot \frac{x}{1 + x}\,\text{V}, \qquad 0 \leq x \leq 9.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $U(0)$, $U(1)$ and $U(9)$.
  \item Find the range $V_U$ for the given interval.
  \item At what $x$ does $U$ become $8\,\text{V}$?
  \item Can $U$ ever reach $12\,\text{V}$? Justify your answer.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.13}{Discharging a capacitor}
The voltage across a discharging capacitor is given by
\[
  u(t) = 10 \cdot e^{-t/2}\,\text{V}, \qquad t \geq 0,
\]
where $t$ is in seconds.
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $u(0)$.
  \item Calculate $u(2)$ and $u(4)$.
  \item Is the function increasing or decreasing?
  \item Find the range $V_u$ for $t \geq 0$.
  \item Does $u(t)$ ever become exactly zero? Justify your answer.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.14}{True or false}
Decide whether the statement is true or false, and justify your answer briefly.
\begin{parts}
  \item The power $P(I) = RI^2$ is negative if the current is negative.
  \item The current $I(R) = \dfrac{12}{R}$ is defined for all $R$.
  \item That $U(2) = 5$ means that $U$ has a zero at $I = 2\,\text{A}$.
  \item If the same voltage $U$ has been measured with two different currents $I$, then $I$ is not a
    function of $U$.
  \item The voltage $u(t) = 3 \cdot 2^t$ (in V) has the initial value $3\,\text{V}$.
\end{parts}

\uppgiftsdel{Part 4}{Mathematics in C}
The exercise below is shown during the lesson, and you can also do it yourself. The program is
written and run in \textbf{OnlineGDB}, \url{https://www.onlinegdb.com/online_c_compiler}, with the

Functions in C are described in \uppref[7]{4.1} of Chapter~7.

\uppgift{4.1}{Functions in C}
The temperature sensor in \secref{c8:sec:typexempel} gives the temperature $f(x) = 100x - 50$
$^{\circ}$C at the input voltage $x$ V, where $0 \leq x \leq 5$. A mathematical function and a
function in C rest on the same idea: one value in, one value out.
\begin{parts}
  \item Compute $f(0)$, $f(0.8)$ and $f(5)$ by hand.
  \item Write a function \code{double temperature(double voltage)} that returns $f(x)$. Then print
    a table of values with $x = 0;\ 0.5;\ 1.0;\ \ldots;\ 5.0$ V and the temperature on each line.
    Hint: let an integer variable \code{i} go from $0$ to $10$ in a loop and compute
    $x = 0.5 \cdot i$.
  \item Solve $f(x) = 100x - 50$ for $x$ and write a function
    \code{double voltage(double temperature)} that works backwards, from temperature to voltage.
    Which voltage gives $30\,^{\circ}$C? Also check that \code{voltage(temperature(0.8))} gives
    $0.8$.
\end{parts}
