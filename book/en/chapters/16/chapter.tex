\chapter{Complex numbers, part II}
\label{c16:ch}

{\large\itshape A formula that unites the exponential function with sine and cosine, and complex
numbers that carry vectors and sinusoidal signals.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Euler's formula and Euler form.
  \item Rectangular form, polar form and Euler form, and when each is easiest to calculate with.
  \item Phasors: sinusoidal signals as complex numbers.
  \item Converting between a phasor and a function of time.
  \item Complex numbers for vector calculations.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter builds on \kapref{15}, where complex numbers were written in rectangular and polar
form. Here you meet Euler's formula, which links the complex exponential function with cosine and
sine and gives a third way of writing a complex number, Euler form. It makes multiplication and
division as simple as in polar form, and it is the key to phasors: complex numbers that represent
sinusoidal voltages and currents. The chapter also shows how vectors in the plane can be written
and calculated with as complex numbers. You may like to read about Euler's formula at
\url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler's_formula} before you start.

\Secref{c16:sec:euler} to \secref{c16:sec:typexempel} are the chapter's theory, with worked
examples. \Secref{c16:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the formulas and what you should be able to
do after the chapter, and \secref{c16:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. The angles are sometimes
given in degrees and sometimes in radians, so keep track of which mode your calculator is in.
