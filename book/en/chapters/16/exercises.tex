\uppgiftsdel{Part 1}{Review exercises}

\uppgift{1.1}{Current in an RL circuit}
A resistor $R = 100\,\Omega$ and an inductor $L = 1\,\text{mH}$ are connected in series and
supplied with the voltage $U = 10\angle 0^{\circ}\,\text{V}$, at the frequency
$f = 50\,\text{kHz}$. The impedance of the inductor is
\[
  Z_L = j\omega L = j2\pi f L.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate the impedance $Z_L$ of the inductor. Keep $\pi$ in the answer and round only at
    the end.
  \item Calculate the total impedance $Z = R + Z_L$ of the circuit in both rectangular and polar
    form.
  \item Calculate the current $I = \dfrac{U}{Z}$ in both polar and rectangular form.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{1.2}{Current in an RC circuit}
The inductor in \uppref{1.1} is replaced by a capacitor $C = 10\,\text{nF}$. The voltage
$U = 10\angle 0^{\circ}\,\text{V}$, the frequency $f = 50\,\text{kHz}$ and the resistor
$R = 100\,\Omega$ are the same. The impedance of the capacitor is
\[
  Z_C = \frac{1}{j\omega C} = -\frac{j}{2\pi f C}.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate the impedance $Z_C$ of the capacitor. Keep $\pi$ in the answer and round only at
    the end.
  \item Calculate the total impedance $Z = R + Z_C$ of the circuit in both rectangular and polar
    form.
  \item Calculate the current $I = \dfrac{U}{Z}$ in both polar and rectangular form.
  \item Compare the current with the current in \uppref{1.1}. What is nearly the same, and what
    differs?
\end{parts}

\uppgiftsdel{Part 2}{New material}

\uppgift{2.1}{Rectangular form to Euler form}
A voltage $u(t)$ in an AC circuit can be represented by a phasor $U$, which in rectangular form
is written
\[
  U = -12 + j4\,\text{V}.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item Draw the phasor $U$ in the complex plane (the $x$-axis is the real part, the $y$-axis the
    imaginary part).
  \item Express the phasor $U$ in Euler form, that is, find the magnitude $\abs{U}$ and the phase
    angle $\delta$ such that $U = \abs{U}e^{j\delta}$.
  \item Assume that the frequency of the voltage is $f = 100\,\text{Hz}$. Find the angular
    frequency $\omega$.
  \item Write the corresponding complex function of time for the voltage in Euler form, that is,
    $u(t) = \abs{U}e^{j(\omega t + \delta)}$.
  \item Draw the sine wave of the voltage $u(t) = \abs{U}\sin(\omega t + \delta)$ over one period
    $T$.
\end{parts}
\note[Note] The phasor $U$ is the complex voltage $u(t)$ without its time dependence.

\uppgift{2.2}{Euler form to rectangular form}
A current $i(t)$ in an AC circuit is described by the complex function of time
\[
  i(t) = 10e^{j(100\pi t - 3\pi/4)}\,\text{mA}.
\]
\begin{parts}
  \item State the magnitude $\abs{I}$, the phase angle $\delta$ (in degrees) and the angular
    frequency $\omega$ of the current.
  \item Find the frequency $f$ of the current.
  \item Write the phasor $I = \abs{I}e^{j\delta}$ in rectangular form.
  \item Draw the phasor $I$ in the complex plane (the $x$-axis is the real part, the $y$-axis the
    imaginary part).
  \item Draw the sine wave of the current $i(t) = \abs{I}\sin(\omega t + \delta)$ over one period
    $T$.
\end{parts}
\note[Note] The exercise goes the opposite way to \uppref{2.1}, from Euler form to rectangular
form.

\uppgiftsdel{Part 3}{Extra exercises}
The exercises below are extra practice, best done on your own after the lesson. Most of them can
be done in your head or with pencil and paper.

\uppgift{3.1}{Euler's formula}
Write in rectangular form.
\begin{delar}(5)
  \task $e^{j\pi/6}$
  \task $e^{j\pi/4}$
  \task $e^{j2\pi/3}$
  \task $e^{-j\pi/3}$
  \task $e^{-j3\pi/4}$
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.2}{Euler form to rectangular form}
Write in rectangular form.
\begin{delar}(4)
  \task $z = 4e^{j\pi/6}$
  \task $z = 5e^{j\pi/3}$
  \task $z = 2e^{-j\pi/4}$
  \task $z = 10e^{j2\pi/3}$
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.3}{Rectangular form to Euler form}
Write in Euler form $\abs{z}e^{j\delta}$.
\begin{delar}(4)
  \task $z = 3 + j3$
  \task $z = -2 + j2$
  \task $z = 1 - j\sqrt{3}$
  \task $z = -4 - j3$
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.4}{Multiplication and division in polar form}
Calculate with $z_1 = 4\,\angle\,30^{\circ}$ and $z_2 = 3\,\angle\,45^{\circ}$:
\begin{delar}(3)
  \task $z_1z_2$
  \task $\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}$
  \task $z_1^2$
  \task* Why is polar form more convenient than rectangular form for multiplication?
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.5}{Comparing the methods}
The complex numbers $z_1 = 3 + j4$ and $z_2 = 1 + j$ are given.
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $z_1z_2$ in rectangular form.
  \item Write both numbers in polar form.
  \item Multiply the numbers in polar form.
  \item Check that the answers to a) and c) are the same number.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.6}{Division in polar form}
Calculate.
\begin{delar}(3)
  \task $\dfrac{10\,\angle\,80^{\circ}}{2\,\angle\,20^{\circ}}$
  \task $\dfrac{12\,\angle\,0^{\circ}}{4\,\angle\,90^{\circ}}$
  \task $\dfrac{6\,\angle\,{-30^{\circ}}}{3\,\angle\,60^{\circ}}$
  \task* What happens to the phase angle when you divide by $j$?
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.7}{Powers of complex numbers}
The number $z = 2\,\angle\,30^{\circ}$ is given.
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $z^2$.
  \item Calculate $z^3$, and write the answer in rectangular form as well.
  \item Calculate $z^4$.
  \item State the general rule for $z^n$ in polar form.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.8}{Phasor from a function of time}
Give the phasor in polar form.
\begin{delar}(2)
  \task $u(t) = 10\sin\left(100\pi t + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)$
  \task $u(t) = 4\sin\left(200\pi t - \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$
  \task* $i(t) = 6\sin(50t)$
  \task* What is required for two phasors to be added?
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.9}{Function of time from a phasor}
The angular frequency is $\omega = 100\pi\,\text{rad/s}$. Write the corresponding function of time.
\begin{delar}(4)
  \task $U = 8\,\angle\,45^{\circ}\,\text{V}$
  \task $U = 5\,\angle\,{-90^{\circ}}\,\text{V}$
  \task $I = 3 + j4\,\text{mA}$
  \task $I = -2 - j2\,\text{mA}$
\end{delar}

\uppgift{3.10}{Phasor addition}
Two voltages with the same frequency are given by $U_1 = 6\,\angle\,0^{\circ}\,\text{V}$ and
$U_2 = 8\,\angle\,90^{\circ}\,\text{V}$.
\begin{parts}
  \item Write both in rectangular form.
  \item Calculate the sum $U = U_1 + U_2$.
  \item Give the sum in polar form.
  \item Write $u(t)$ if $\omega = 100\pi\,\text{rad/s}$.
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.11}{Impedance from voltage and current}
A circuit has $U = 20\,\angle\,60^{\circ}\,\text{V}$ and $I = 4\,\angle\,15^{\circ}\,\text{A}$.
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate the impedance $Z = \dfrac{U}{I}$ in polar form.
  \item Write $Z$ in rectangular form.
  \item Is the circuit inductive or capacitive?
  \item What are the resistance and the reactance?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.12}{Impedance of an inductor and a capacitor}
At $\omega = 1000\,\text{rad/s}$, $L = 20\,\text{mH}$ and $C = 50\,\mu\text{F}$.
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $Z_L = j\omega L$.
  \item Calculate $Z_C = -\dfrac{j}{\omega C}$.
  \item A resistor $R = 15\,\Omega$ is connected in series with both. Calculate the total
    impedance.
  \item What is this condition called?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.13}{Rotation by $j$}
The number $z = 3 + j4$ is given.
\begin{parts}
  \item Calculate $jz$.
  \item Compare $\abs{z}$ and $\abs{jz}$.
  \item Compare the phase angles of the two numbers.
  \item What does multiplication by $j$ do geometrically?
\end{parts}

\uppgift{3.14}{True or false}
Decide whether each statement is true or false, and justify your answer briefly.
\begin{parts}
  \item Two complex numbers with the same magnitude are always equal.
  \item $\abs{z_1z_2} = \abs{z_1} + \abs{z_2}$
  \item In division, the phase angles are subtracted.
  \item A phasor contains information about the frequency of the signal.
  \item The phase angle of $z = a + jb$ is always $\tan^{-1}\frac{b}{a}$.
  \item Multiplying complex numbers is easiest in rectangular form.
\end{parts}

\uppgiftsdel{Part 4}{Mathematics in C}
The teacher shows the exercise below during the lesson if time allows, in VS Code and with
\code{make}. You can
also do it yourself in \textbf{OnlineGDB}, \url{https://www.onlinegdb.com/online_c_compiler}, with
the language set to \textbf{C}. The struct \code{complex_t} and the functions from \uppref[15]{4.1}
of Chapter~15 are also used here.

\uppgift{4.1}{Rotation by $j$ in C}
The exercise builds on \uppref{3.13}. The number $z = 3 + j4$ is given.
\begin{parts}
  \item Compute $jz$, $j^2 z$, $j^3 z$ and $j^4 z$ by hand.
  \item Show that $j(a + jb) = -b + ja$. Then write the function \code{complex_t
    complex_rotate_j(const complex_t* z)}, which returns $jz$. Multiply $z$ by $j$ four times in a
    loop, with \code{z = complex_rotate_j(&z);}. Print the number in rectangular form and its
    argument in degrees before the first multiplication and after each multiplication.
  \item How much does the argument change with each multiplication? Why is $j^4 z = z$?
  \item Change the call to \code{complex_rotate_j(&z);}, without \code{z =}. What is printed now?
    Explain why, given that the parameter is \code{const complex_t*}.
\end{parts}
