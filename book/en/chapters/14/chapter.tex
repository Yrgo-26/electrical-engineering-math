\chapter{Integrals}
\label{c14:ch}

{\large\itshape The derivative in reverse: antiderivatives, the area under a curve, and the charge
that a current carries.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What an integral is and how it is calculated.
  \item Antiderivatives and the constant of integration.
  \item Rules of integration for the most common functions.
  \item Definite integrals and area.
  \item Charge from current and energy from power.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

In \kapref{12} and \kapref{13} we differentiated functions to find how fast they change. This
chapter goes the other way: given a function, we look for a function whose derivative it is, an
antiderivative. The chapter goes through the rules of integration for the most common functions,
how the definite integral gives the area under a curve, and how the constant of integration is
found from an initial condition. The application in electrical engineering is direct: charge is
the integral of current, and the energy stored in an inductor is the integral of power.

\Secref{c14:sec:primitiv} to \secref{c14:sec:laddning} are the chapter's theory, and
\secref{c14:sec:typexempel} works through four examples. \Secref{c14:sec:sammanfattning}
summarises the formulas and what you should be able to do after the chapter, and
\secref{c14:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. As preparation, you can watch a short video about
integrals as a concept: \url{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=__Uw1SXPW7s}.
