\chapter{Linear equations and inequalities}
\label{c3:ch}

{\large\itshape Finding the unknown: equations, inequalities and absolute values.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Linear equations and the principles for solving them.
  \item Proportions.
  \item Inequalities and solution intervals on the number line.
  \item Equations with absolute values.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

\Kapref{2} simplified and factorised expressions. This chapter sets two expressions equal to each
other and solves the equation that results. A linear equation is solved with two principles, the
addition principle and the multiplication principle, and the same principles solve inequalities,
with one important exception: the inequality sign reverses when you multiply or divide by a
negative number. The chapter also covers proportions and equations with absolute values. The
applications recur throughout the course: solving Ohm's law for an unknown resistance, finding the
safe operating area of a component and expressing a tolerance with an absolute value.

\Secref{c3:sec:ekvationer} to \secref{c3:sec:absolutbelopp} are the chapter's theory, with
examples. \Secref{c3:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the rules and what you should be able to do
after the chapter, and \secref{c3:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. Most of them can be done in
your head or with pencil and paper.
