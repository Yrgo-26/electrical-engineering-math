\chapter{Algebra}
\label{c2:ch}

{\large\itshape Calculating with letters: simplifying, expanding and factorising.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Algebraic expressions and variables.
  \item Simplifying algebraic expressions.
  \item The distributive law and expanding brackets.
  \item The squaring rules and the difference of two squares.
  \item Factorisation.
  \item Ohm's law and the power formulas in algebraic form.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

In electrical engineering, almost every relationship is written with letters: Ohm's law $U = RI$,
the power $P = UI$ and the resistance of a series connection $R_{\text{TOT}} = R_1 + R_2$. This
chapter is about how to calculate with such expressions. It goes through what variables, terms,
factors and coefficients are, how like terms are collected, how brackets are expanded with the
distributive law, the squaring rules and the difference of two squares, and how an expression is
factorised. The rules are then used on circuits, where the right rewriting often saves work. The
chapter builds on the order of operations and the fractions of \kapref{1}; powers, roots, units and
prefixes come in \kapref{5}, and equations and working with formulas continue in \kapref{3}.

\Secref{c2:sec:variabler} to \secref{c2:sec:tillampning} are the chapter's theory, with examples.
\Secref{c2:sec:sammanfattning} summarises the rules and what you should be able to do after the
chapter, and \secref{c2:sec:uppgifter} holds the exercises. Most of them can be done in your head
or with pencil and paper.
