\svar{2.2} \sv{a} $f'(x) = -2x + 8$ \sv{b} $x = 4$ \sv{c} Maximum, since

\sv{e} A downward-opening parabola with its peak at $(4, 9)$ and zeros at $x = 1$ and $x = 7$.

\svar{2.3} \sv{a} $i'(t) = -0.6t^2 + 3.6t - 3.6$
\sv{b} $t = 3 \pm \sqrt{3}$, so $t_1 \approx 1.27\,\text{s}$ and
$t_2 \approx 4.73\,\text{s}$.
\sv{c} $i''(t_1) \approx 2.08 > 0$: minimum at $t_1$; $i''(t_2) \approx -2.08 < 0$:
maximum at $t_2$.
\sv{d} The minimum value is $i(t_1) \approx 0.42\,\text{A}$ and the maximum value
$i(t_2) \approx 4.58\,\text{A}$.
\sv{e} The curve starts at $i(0) = 2.5\,\text{A}$, has a valley at $(1.27, 0.42)$ and a peak at
$(4.73, 4.58)$, and then falls.

\svar{3.1} \sv{a} $5x^4$ \sv{b} $12x^2$ \sv{c} $0$ \sv{d} $4x - 5$ \sv{e} $-12x^3 + 1$
\sv{f} $x^2$

\svar{3.2} \sv{a} The quotient simplifies to $4 + h$. \sv{b} $4$
\sv{c} $f'(2) = 2 \times 2 = 4$, the same answer. \sv{d} $4.1$ and $4.01$ respectively

\svar{3.3} \sv{a} $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3$ \sv{b} $-3$ \sv{c} $9$
\sv{d} Where $x = -1$ and $x = 1$, that is, at $(-1, 2)$ and $(1, -2)$.

\svar{3.4} \sv{a} $2$ \sv{b} $2$ \sv{c} $y = 2x - 4$ \sv{d} At the point $(2, 0)$.

\svar{3.5} \sv{a} $f'(x) = 2x - 6$ \sv{b} $x = 3$ \sv{c} Minimum, since $f''(x) = 2 > 0$.
\sv{d} $f(3) = -1$; the minimum point is $(3, -1)$.

\svar{3.6} \sv{a} $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 3(x - 3)(x + 1)$ \sv{b} $x = -1$ and $x = 3$
\sv{c} Maximum at $x = -1$ ($f''(-1) = -12 < 0$), minimum at $x = 3$ ($f''(3) = 12 > 0$).
\sv{d} $f(-1) = 10$ and $f(3) = -22$

\svar{3.7} \sv{a} $f'(x) = 2x - 4$ \sv{b} $x > 2$ \sv{c} $x < 2$
\sv{d} $f'(2) = 0$: the function goes from decreasing to increasing, so $x = 2$ is a minimum point.

\svar{3.8} \sv{a} $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 4x$, $f''(x) = 12x^2 - 4$
\sv{b} $f'(x) = 15x^2 + 2$, $f''(x) = 30x$ \sv{c} $f'(x) = -2x + 7$, $f''(x) = -2$
\sv{d} $f''(x) > 0$: the curve is concave up, like a valley; $f''(x) < 0$: concave down, like a
hill.

\svar{3.9} \sv{a} $u'(t) = 8t + 2$ \sv{b} $i_C(t) = (0.8t + 0.2)\,\text{mA}$
\sv{c} $1.0\,\text{mA}$ \sv{d} $t = 2.25\,\text{s}$

\svar{3.10} \sv{a} $i'(t) = 4t - 4$ \sv{b} $u_L(t) = (2t - 2)\,\text{V}$ \sv{c} $t = 1\,\text{s}$
\sv{d} The current is stationary, a minimum of $i(1) = 1\,\text{A}$: for an instant it stops
changing.

\svar{3.11} \sv{a} $P'(I) = 24 - 6I$ \sv{b} $I = 4\,\text{A}$
\sv{c} Maximum, since $P''(I) = -6 < 0$. \sv{d} $P(4) = 48\,\text{W}$

\svar{3.12} \sv{a} $u'(t) = -4t + 12$ \sv{b} At $t = 3\,\text{s}$, when $u = 18\,\text{V}$.
\sv{c} It is increasing by $8\,\text{V/s}$. \sv{d} $t = 4\,\text{s}$

\svar{3.13} \sv{a} Increasing, since $f'(1) > 0$. \sv{b} Decreasing, since $f'(3) < 0$.
\sv{c} A maximum point: $f'(2) = 0$ and $f''(2) < 0$.
\sv{d} The graph rises up to $x = 2$, has a horizontal tangent at the peak, and then falls.

\svar{3.14} \sv{a} No extra $x$ should remain: $f'(x) = 3x^2$.
\sv{b} A constant has the derivative zero: $f'(x) = 0$.
\sv{c} The derivative of $4x$ is the slope: $f'(x) = 4$.
\sv{d} The term $2x$ must be differentiated too: $f'(x) = 6x + 2$.
\sv{e} False: a stationary point can also be a minimum or a stationary point of inflection.
\sv{f} False: $f''(x)$ is found by differentiating $f'(x)$ once more. For example, $f(x) = x^3$
gives $f''(x) = 6x$, not $9x^4$.
